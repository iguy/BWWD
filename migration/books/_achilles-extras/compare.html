Iliad vs. The Song of Achilles

Homer and Madeline Miller tell much of the same story. What changes is who is speaking, how much time we see, and what the story is really about.

The Iliad (Homer)The Song of Achilles (Miller)
Who tells itA bard calling on the Muse, seeing everyone from abovePatroclus, in the first person, and after his death as a ghost
Time spanAbout fifty days in the tenth year of the warPatroclus's childhood through Achilles's death and beyond
What it's aboutThe rage of Achilles and what it costs the GreeksThe love between Achilles and Patroclus, and what glory costs it
Achilles & PatroclusDevoted companions; the nature of their bond is left openOpenly lovers, from boyhood friendship onward
PatroclusAn older, skilled warrior and Achilles's right handA gentle exile who learns medicine and would rather heal than fight
The godsOn stage constantly, taking sides and steering battlesMostly offstage; Thetis is the god who matters
BriseisA prize of war at the centre of a quarrel, with little voiceA friend to Patroclus, with her own fears and courage
HectorA full hero in his own right: son, husband, father, defender of TroySeen from the Greek camp, mostly as the man Achilles is fated to kill
How it endsHector's funeral in Troy; Achilles's death is still to comeAchilles's death, and the two lovers finally reunited in the underworld
StyleEpic verse, repeated phrases and epithets, made to be performed aloudSpare, lyrical modern prose, made to be read close
