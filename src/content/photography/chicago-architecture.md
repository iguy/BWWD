<p>Chicago, June 2025. I was in the city for a wellness weekend, but the best of it was the Chicago Architecture Center's walking tour of the Loop and its river cruise.</p>

<h2>The Loop on foot</h2>

<p>The Reliance Building on State Street (Burnham & Root, 1895) is mostly glass, held in a thin skin of white terracotta. A steel frame carries the load, so the walls don't have to. It's one of the earliest curtain walls, and a direct ancestor of the glass towers around it.</p>

<p>A few blocks south, the Palmer House's State Street entrance has brass peacock doors, a nod to C.D. Peacock, the jewelers who kept a store in the building for decades.</p>

<h2>The Cultural Center</h2>

<p>The Chicago Cultural Center (Shepley, Rutan and Coolidge, 1897) was built as the city's central public library, and every surface inside is mosaic, marble or glass. It has two stained-glass domes. The one in the old Grand Army of the Republic hall is by Healy & Millet. The one in Preston Bradley Hall is the largest Tiffany glass dome in the world, 38 feet across.</p>

<h2>From the river</h2>

<p>The Architecture Center's river cruise was the highlight of the trip, and I'd recommend it to anyone visiting. Shooting from a moving boat, you don't choose your angle. You look up and wait for the pattern to line up.</p>

<p>Marina City (Bertrand Goldberg, early 1960s) is two 65-story towers. The bottom 19 floors of each are a spiral parking garage, and every apartment above ends in a semicircular balcony. That's the scalloped pattern in the first photo.</p>

<p>Chicago has 37 movable bridges, more than any other city. Most are Chicago-style trunnion bascules: each leaf pivots on an axle, balanced by a counterweight in a pit under the riverbank. From below, the open steel deck turns sunlight into a lattice of shadow.</p>
